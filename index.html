N25 3D Printing Room
01 / Site Protocol

The Room Already Had a Protocol

In the N25 3D printing room, the use of each box was mostly fixed. There were clean beds for printing, tools for removing prints, and a place for finished prints. However, before the intervention, these territories were not clearly separated enough.

CLEAN BEDS

Clean printer beds for starting a new print.

TOOLS

Scrapers, hammers, and tools for removing prints from the bed.

FINISHED PRINTS

Temporary storage for prints removed by someone else.

02 / Before

Mixed Territory

Before the intervention, tools, small prints, support scraps, and printer beds shared the same visual field. The problem was not simply that the room was messy. The problem was that the boundaries between different actions were weak.

Before

The room had containers, but the territories were not clear enough.

Prototype

I first tested whether a small shelf and simple labels could change where objects were placed.

03 / Intervention

Making Territories

I rearranged the space by adding a small shelf, separating containers, and later adding labels. The goal was not to create a completely new rule, but to make the existing categories physically and visually separate.

Prototype

Temporary shelf + simple name tags

The first prototype made the room easier to read. Fewer support scraps and random parts were placed in the tool box. However, the shelf was too small, and small prints could fall through the gap.

First Installation

Custom shelf made with basket and tension rods

After the purchased shelf did not fit the space, I made a new shelf using a storage basket and tension rods. Even without name tags, the rough division between tools, beds, and prints seemed to remain.

Name Tags Added

Labels + board marker

Following the critique, I added editable labels and placed a board marker nearby. However, no one tried to rewrite the labels. I realized that the intervention did not clearly invite participation yet.

04 / Observation

What Changed?

The intervention showed that arrangement can change behavior, but only up to a point. It guided where people placed objects, but it could not completely change what people felt allowed to touch.

Observation 1

The tool box became cleaner.

After the prototype, fewer support scraps and small parts were placed in the tool box.

Observation 2

Separation worked even before labels.

Once the space was divided, the rough separation between tools, beds, and prints seemed to remain even before clear name tags were added.

Observation 3

Some boundaries were social, not spatial.

People still tended to leave other people’s prints attached to the bed. A shelf can suggest where finished prints should go, but it cannot fully remove the hesitation of touching someone else’s fragile work.

Without Tags

The physical separation already began to guide behavior.

With Tags

Labels made the territories more readable, but participation still needed invitation.

05 / Finding

Space Can Suggest, But Not Force

The arrangement guided small actions: where to put tools, where to return beds, and where not to place scraps. But it also revealed a limit. Spatial boundaries can make behavior easier, but they do not automatically solve social hesitation.

I did not tell people what to do. I rearranged the space and watched what changed.
06 / Tap the Space

07 / Visitor Participation

Recommend a Better Arrangement

My arrangement changed some behaviors, but not all of them. If you use this room, you may know better than I do: what kind of area, label, or container would make the protocol clearer?

FRAGILE PRINTS

ask the owner before removing

This interaction does not save or share your input. It only lets you test how a new label could change the way this space is read.

08 / Reflection

Arrangement as Instruction

This project is not simply about organizing a room. It asks how much behavior can be guided by the configuration of space.

A shelf is not just storage.
A box is not just a container.
A label is not just a name.
Together, they become a quiet instruction.
